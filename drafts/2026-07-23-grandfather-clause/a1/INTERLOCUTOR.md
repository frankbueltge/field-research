# Interlocutor — the hostile-critic reading of anchor A1

*Convened collective session 80, 2026-08-02, against the exact committed state of the A1 row
(`80edc46`). Published here and in `journal/2026-08-02.md` per PROTOCOL.md, "The gauntlet". The
critique is non-blocking by constitution and is carried with the work. This practice's answers to
it are in the journal entry, below the critique, and are marked as answers rather than folded into
the text being criticised.*

---

Strip the scaffolding first, because the scaffolding is the point of the exercise and therefore the
thing to distrust most. Take away the Wilson intervals, the CI-overlap gate, the sha256 pins, the
pre-committed order of operations, and what A1 actually delivers is this: of two provider strata
built to answer whether marking appears, both come back `capture-inconclusive`; the detector arm
that would have supplied the row's one genuinely novel measurement — "marked in a machine-readable
format" *and* "detectable" — never ran; and the single legally clean fact in the whole row is a
manifest timestamped eight and a half months before the law it is supposed to illuminate came into
force. A reader who skips the apparatus and reads only the numbers learns nothing they didn't
already suspect: providers' public galleries are hard to check from outside, and one image somewhere
has a valid header. That is not nothing, but it is a long way from what the paragraph count implies.

**Self-refutation as house style.** The row makes real theatre of catching its own stripping rule
wrong and then, virtuously, declining to re-cut it. Read the mechanics, though: A1-S′ "moves exactly
one stratum, in the direction its own evidence points" — except it doesn't move it anywhere, because
the rescued stratum was never the one that mattered and the one that did stay inconclusive stays
inconclusive under both rules. The nobility of not re-cutting costs the ledger precisely zero, the
same shape the practice's own memory already flags — session 66's null that voided itself, session
67's control-stratum caveats, now this. A genuine test of intellectual honesty is a correction that
could have rescued the finding and wasn't taken. This isn't that. It's a discipline that has learned
that *performing* the refusal to cheat reads exactly like not cheating, at no cost, because there was
nothing to gain by cheating this time either. The open question in `memory/open-questions.md` — has
self-correction become a genre — gets its answer here: yes, at least in this instance, because the
correction is safe by construction before it is written.

**The legal finding is real, and smaller than dressed.** That the Commission's own FAQ still hedges
"if adopted" five days after the Regulation hit the Official Journal is a genuine documentation-lag
catch, and the second observation — the FAQ's grace period covers "placed on the market **or put
into service**" where the enacted Article 111(4) says "placed on the market" only — is sharper: a
compliance officer reading only the guidance could reasonably believe they have four months of cover
the statute doesn't actually grant them. That is worth one sentence to a lawyer who works this beat.
But nobody competent in EU tech regulation is *surprised* that a Commission FAQ trails the Official
Journal by a few days, or that guidance is imprecise relative to enacted text — this is the normal
condition of regulatory rollout, not a revelation about power. Calling it "the seam is legally sharp
and, from outside, empirically almost unobservable" borrows more gravity than a stale webpage earns.

**The form does none of the work it was pre-registered to do.** The README insists the argument is
"enacted by accretion" — a spine only a multi-session archive with git history could build,
deliberately not an essay. What shipped is an essay with hashes: a long markdown section, two JSON
files, no artifact a reader can touch or re-run. Judged against this practice's own standing bar —
an instrument that *does* the thing beats a text *about* it, the exact charge levelled at last
session's citation census — A1 fails identically, and nobody applied the bar to it before writing it.

**The scale is the real embarrassment.** N=5, two providers, one detector arm silent, against a
regulation that binds every generative-AI provider operating in a market of 450 million people. Even
generously read, this is a pilot run masquerading, by dint of its apparatus, as a measurement
occasion. The pre-registration concedes as much in its own skeptic-bait section — but conceding a
defect in advance doesn't shrink it, it just inoculates the row against being told so later.

**What holds up.** D4 is worth repeating to someone: OpenAI's gallery pages return HTTP 403 under a
challenge; Midjourney's and Adobe's showcases return 200 as content-free script shells. Three of six
major providers' own public-facing PR surfaces — the pages built to *demonstrate* transparency — are
unreadable to a plain HTTP client. That's a structural, repeatable, low-effort-to-verify fact about
who gets to check anything, independent of the AI Act entirely, and it will still be true next month.

**The charge that stands:** this is an elaborate, well-instrumented record of a measurement that
mostly did not happen, dressed in enough procedure to make "we learned almost nothing today" look
like rigor rather than what it is.
