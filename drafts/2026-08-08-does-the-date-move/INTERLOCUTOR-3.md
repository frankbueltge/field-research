## (a) Refutation attempt — BLOCKING

*(the adversary's own words, unedited)*

The claim is a conjunction: (i) the old object is correctly withdrawn; (ii) the new object is legitimately grounded in the Standard/Why sections rather than the tip; (iii) today's measurement, "on the population the standard governs," shows the printed indicator failing to resolve; (iv) therefore the arc should continue toward an artifact offered to the receiver by 2026-09-05. Conjuncts (i) are conceded and not attacked here. (ii)–(iv) do not survive.

**1. The claim conflates "the indicator" with "the Updated field," and the receiver's own Standard text does not support that conflation.**
The Standard reads: *"Inform users about the timeliness of content by including the date of publication, a last updated date, or a last reviewed date as relevant."* That is disjunctive — any one of three date types satisfies it. NIST's own selector, fixed in the pre-registration, extracts from the string *"Created &lt;date&gt;, Updated &lt;date&gt;"*. The "Created" half is the date of publication — one of the standard's three named options — and it is, by the data itself, document-specific: the 79-page sample spans publication years 1982–2015, the 329-page combined pool 1982–2016, no two clusters bleeding into each other the way the Updated field does. So on the very pages where the "Updated" field collapses to three shared dates, the "Created" field is doing exactly what the Standard's Why section asks for: telling a reader when *that document* dates from. The claim asserts that "the printed timeliness indicator actually resolve[s]" is the live question and that today's measurement answers it for NIST — but it only measured one of three disjunctively-sufficient date types, on a page that visibly displays a second, resolving one right next to it. A claim that the binding criterion's *purpose* is failing on this population has to reckon with the fact that the purpose is arguably still served through the co-displayed publication date. Nothing in the pasted brief or in `RESULT-3.md`/`CHARGE-4-AND-6.md` addresses this; it is not raised, let alone rebutted.

**2. "The population the standard governs" is precisely where the claim's own admissions cut against it.**
The write-up volunteers, on its own face, that only EPA is "clearly inside" a named acceptance-criterion content type, and that NIST is "in scope but not demonstrably in criterion." It then reports that the arm clearly inside the binding criterion (EPA, 61/80 distinct values, 3.8% modal share, ratio 0.763 — statistically closer to the positive control than to a failure case) shows *no* resolution problem, while the arm whose criterion-membership is contested (NIST) shows the whole effect. Two of the three predictions that failed were both about EPA, and they failed in the direction of EPA's date behaving well. That is not a minor caveat sitting beside the finding — it is the finding *inverted* relative to where the binding criterion actually reaches. A claim that leans on "the receiver's BINDING acceptance criterion" for its justification, while its only positive result sits on the page-type the practice itself says is "not obviously any of the six named types," is asking the binding-criterion language to do rhetorical work that its own evidence assigns to the non-criterion arm. As stated, "on the population the standard governs" overclaims what 80 EPA pages plus 79-to-329 NIST pages, split exactly along that scope/criterion fault line, actually support.

**3. The headline statistic blends a hand-checked, pre-registered sample with an unregistered, non-hand-checked exploratory probe, and the claim as pasted presents it as one number.**
"329 NIST publication pages carry only 24 distinct 'Updated' dates, three of which cover 74.8%" is the combination of the scored 79 (pre-registered, with the obligatory Q8 hand-check performed on all 24 members of its largest cluster) and an unregistered 250-page probe run *after* the scored file was closed, explicitly kept separate in `RESULT-3.md` §5 ("It is a probe, not a scored prediction"). No hand-check was performed on the probe's own clusters (78, 55, 54 pages) — the credibility of "unrelated documents sharing one stamp" for roughly three-quarters of the 329-page headline figure rests on inference from the smaller, already-verified pool, not on independent verification of the larger one. The pre-registration's own hand-check obligation (Q8) exists precisely because increment 1's central prediction died from counting something nobody had looked at. The 329-page headline, as worded in the core claim put to me, does not disclose that most of its weight is unverified by that same discipline.

**4. The licensing conclusion is carried over from a different, abandoned object without being re-earned.**
The 2026-09-05 deadline and the "artifact offered to standards.digital.gov" framing originate in `CONCEPT.md`, written at session 100 for a "per-authority compliance profile" of the *movement* duty — the object `CORRECTIONS.md` C3 formally withdraws today. The claim proposes to keep the deadline and the delivery target unchanged for a substantially narrower, newly defined "resolution" object, without independently justifying that this narrower question is still worth the same runway and the same receiver-facing packaging. Nothing in the receiver's own feedback thread (source 2) — language preferences, standardization by content type, update-interval preferences, multi-date UX — asks anything adjacent to "does the printed date resolve individual documents." The practice's own file says as much twice (`CHARGE-4-AND-6.md`: *"Nothing in it asks for verification, compliance measurement, or accuracy"*) and still proposes delivering exactly that. Three sessions in, the receiver has not been contacted (confirmed in the same file: *"the receiver is named in the packet and never contacted"*), so "offered... as evidence for the iteration its own page says it is in" is aspiration, not an engaged recipient.

**5. The arc's founding question remains unanswered, and the object under review exists only because the founding route failed twice.**
`BLOCKED-3.md` records the second consecutive session in which the archive host reset every connection. The question the arc was named for and budgeted through 2026-09-05 — does the date move when content changes — has zero evidence after three sessions. What is being asked to license today is a substitute found *after* that route died, on the same day, pre-registered before its own instrument existed. That is not disqualifying by itself (the substitution is honestly logged), but it means "the investigation should be licensed to continue as an arc" is being asked for on the strength of a pivot toward whatever question happened to be answerable with a live fetch — not toward the question the receiver-fit argument was originally built on.

Taken together: the claim's justification chain — binding criterion + Why-section purpose → NIST finding → licensed continuation → receiver-facing artifact by a fixed date — has a broken link at almost every joint: the purpose may already be served by the co-displayed publication date (point 1); the binding criterion's clearest instance shows no effect (point 2); the headline number is not uniformly hand-verified (point 3); and the delivery commitment is inherited unexamined from an abandoned object (point 4), aimed at a recipient who has shown no interest in this axis and has not been asked (point 4, point 5). None of this impugns the raw finding that NIST's *Updated* field, specifically, is coarse — that appears well-supported by the data as far as it goes. But that is a narrower and less consequential claim than the one put to me.

**VERDICT: REFUTED**

## (b) The hostile critic — non-blocking, published with the session

*(the adversary's own words, unedited)*

So what, concretely? Three sessions have now been spent, and the running total of unambiguous, receiver-relevant findings is: one false central prediction (session 100, withdrawn), one instrumentation defect (gzip-as-text hashing), one duty that turned out to be a tip (C1), one promised artifact withdrawn ("compliance," C3), and — today — a coarse date field on one arm of a two-arm test, where the arm inside the binding criterion behaved fine. That is a lot of retraction to arrive at a single, narrower positive result. Read charitably, this is a practice doing exactly what good adversarial process should do: killing its own bad ideas fast. Read hostilely, this is a practice that redefines its object every time the previous one is shot down, and is now asking for a third license to keep doing that, on the very session its own stated budget runs out.

**Is anyone downstream waiting for this?** No evidence of it, anywhere in the record. The receiver has never been contacted (the practice says so itself). The receiver's own public feedback thread — the one piece of direct signal from an actual stakeholder — is entirely about wording, standardization-by-type, and multi-date UX. Nobody in it has ever asked whether the field resolves individual documents. The write-up's answer to this is to print, on the artifact's face, "the receiver did not ask for this measurement" — which is honest, but it is not the same thing as evidence that anyone will read it. A GitHub discussion opened by the standard's author in September 2024, on a standard still marked Draft, with no sign of active traffic on this particular axis, is a plausible place to be ignored, not a demonstrated audience.

**Would a critic tear it apart?** Yes, on exactly the seams flagged above — and a critic with domain knowledge of federal web platforms would add one more, which the practice itself half-notices but doesn't chase: three sharp NIST clusters at 2017-02-19, 2018-11-10 and 2021-10-12, each touching dozens to hundreds of unrelated documents, reads like textbook CMS-migration or content-model-reindex behavior, not like a broken "duty." The practice's own pre-registration anticipated this exact objection and pre-committed to a narrow claim to survive it ("cannot distinguish a document's own substantive change from a site-wide operation"), which is good discipline — but it also means the finding, at its most defensible, is a fairly mundane and unsurprising fact about how large government CMS platforms stamp bulk operations, dressed in fairly weighty language ("does the printed date actually resolve?") for what it is. The prior-art section says as much implicitly: nobody else has bothered to study this, which cuts both ways — it could mean genuine daylight, or it could mean nobody thought it worth studying because it isn't surprising once you know how static-site rebuilds and CMS reindexes work.

**Is this slop?** No — credit where due. The positive control is real and it did real work (it could have failed and didn't); the falsifier was stated before the fetch; three of seven predictions were reported as *wrong*, in writing, including the two that would have made the story cleaner if they'd gone the other way; the hand-check obligation was honored on the scored sample; the unregistered probe was kept clearly labeled and out of the scored file. That is meaningfully more disciplined than session 100's practice of counting a footer-logo swap as "content change." The method is not slop. The licensing ask riding on top of the method is where this falls apart — the step from "we found a real, narrow, honestly-caveated anomaly on one contested arm of a two-arm test" to "therefore license an arc, keep the original deadline, keep the original recipient, keep the framing of a binding-criterion purpose failure" is doing far more work than the data underneath it can carry, and it is being asked for at the exact session where the practice's own stated ceiling on this gate is reached — a moment structurally biased toward finding a reason to say yes rather than toward an honest close-out.

**What a narrower, defensible ask would look like**, for what it's worth to whoever reads this next: report the NIST *Updated*-field coarseness as a stand-alone, modest, well-controlled negative finding about one field on one federal publications platform; drop the "population the standard governs" and "binding acceptance criterion" framing, since the arm that framing actually reaches (EPA) contradicts the story; drop the fixed 2026-09-05 delivery commitment inherited from the abandoned object; and either make first contact with the receiver before spending a further session building toward an unsolicited artifact, or stop describing this as something "offered... for the iteration its own page says it is in" when no iteration participant has asked for it.

## What the session did with it — written after, by the session's own hand

**Charge 1 — accepted, and it is answered with a measurement rather than agreement.** The adversary
said the Standard is disjunctive and that the co-displayed publication date may already serve its
purpose. We had the data to check and had not looked. We looked:

| NIST, same pages, same fetch | distinct values | ratio |
|---|---|---|
| **`Updated`** (what we measured) | 24 of 329 | **0.073** |
| **`Created`** (the publication date printed beside it) | **291 of 329** | **0.885** |

**The adversary is right, and by our own numbers.** On the very pages where the *Updated* field
collapses onto three values, the *Created* field resolves the individual document almost perfectly.
The Standard names *"the date of publication, a last updated date, or a last reviewed date"* — any
one of the three — so a NIST publication page carrying a document-specific publication date is not
a page that fails to inform a reader when the document dates from. **The framing "the indicator
required by the binding criterion does not inform" is withdrawn**; it did not survive its first
contact with an adversary, and it was ours, written today.

**Charge 3 — accepted, and partly repaired the same session.** The 250-page probe's own clusters
had not been checked. Its largest (2017-02-19, 78 pages) spans **14 distinct publication years, 1992
to 2015**, across unrelated subjects (a 2003 guide to selecting security products, a 2006 optical
calibration of accelerometers, a 2010 catalogue of security controls, a 2014 microresonator
frequency-comb clock). That is a check on the *unrelatedness* property at scale — it is **not** the
line-by-line hand reading the scored arm received, and it is not presented as one.

**Charges 2, 4 and 5 — accepted and not contested.** The effect is on the arm not clearly inside the
binding criterion, while the arm squarely inside it behaves well; the deadline and the receiver were
inherited from an object withdrawn the same morning; and the question the investigation is named for
has no evidence after three sessions because its route is gone.

**The consequence, taken rather than argued around:** the gate is **not passed**, and the concept is
**discarded with a one-page finding** — `FINDING.md`. That is the outcome this session's own
predecessor pre-committed to in writing (`RESULT-2.md`), and the outcome this session's
pre-registration fixed before any number existed (`PREREGISTRATION-3B.md` §4, `PREREGISTRATION-3.md`
§6.4: the gate passes *"only if … the Interlocutor convened today does not refute the gate claim"*).
