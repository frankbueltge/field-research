# Neighbours checked before the bundle was written — session 120, 2026-08-15

*This practice was caught twice in one week claiming novelty its own record refuted
(sessions 118 and 119). Before writing a receiver-facing bundle whose whole pitch is that
nobody is running the credential-free half, the claim was put against the house's own
catalogues and against the field. What follows is what was found, including the finding that
narrows this arc's own concept.*

All three of the house's catalogues answered on 2026-08-15 (fetched first-hand, never mirrored):
`papers/index.json` **1,116 entries**, `atlas/werke.json` **505 entries**,
`datasets/register.json` **59 entries**.

## 1. The finding that narrows this arc's own claim

`CONCEPT.md` §1 says the missing half is buildable *"at a scale and a constancy no one is
running."* The house's own paper register holds a 2026 work that runs a version of the missing
half:

> **Luka Bekavac, Simon Mayer, "Platforms' Research API Data Access: What Users See vs. What
> Researchers can Retrieve", FAccT '26, 25 June 2026, pp. 8276–8299**
> (`https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3805689.3812237`; publisher page HTTP 403 from here, so the
> bibliographic detail is from a search index and the substance from the authors' own preprint
> of the same work, **arXiv:2601.12390v1**, submitted 2026-01-18,
> `https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12390`, read first-hand).

Their method, in their own framing: they **reconstruct the full information feeds of two
controlled sockpuppet accounts during two election periods** and compare that user-visible
public information environment against what each platform's Research API returns, for the
TikTok Research API and the Meta Content Library. They report exclusion of up to ~50 % of the
public information environment, up to ~83 % metadata loss, and request ceilings as low as
~1,000 per day.

**This is a two-sided comparison, and it is exactly the shape this arc says is missing.** The
concept's sentence as written is therefore too strong and is narrowed here:

- their public-side reconstruction runs **through accounts**, not credential-free;
- it covers **two bounded election periods**, not a running series;
- it is a **published study**, not an instrument a third party can point at a list of their own.

**What survives, and it is what the bundle claims:** no continuously running, credential-free,
dated public-presence reference — one that a stranger can address their own list of identifiers
against, on a day of their choosing, with an age-stratified expectation attached — was found in
1,116 catalogued papers or in the field searches run here. The bundle claims that, and not more.

## 2. The other close neighbours in the house register

- **George D. Pearson et al., "Beyond the margin of error: a systematic and replicable audit of
  the TikTok research API", 2024** (`10.1080/1369118x.2024.2420032`) — an API-side audit;
  no credential-free public control arm.
- **Jonathan Zittrain, John Bowers, Clare Stanton, "The Paper of Record Meets an Ephemeral Web:
  An Examination of Linkrot and Content Drift within The New York Times", 2021**
  (`10.2139/ssrn.3833133`, catalogue status *verified*) — the canonical neighbour for the *idea*
  of a reference decay rate, on a different object (news links, not platform videos) and as a
  one-off study rather than a running series.
- **Arkaitz Zubiaga, "A Longitudinal Assessment of the Persistence of Twitter Datasets", 2017**
  (`arXiv:1709.09186`) — the nearest methodological neighbour: measuring, over time, what share
  of a social-platform dataset stays retrievable. Different platform, different era, and again a
  study rather than a maintained instrument. **Not read in full this session; listed as a
  neighbour on its catalogue record, not summarised beyond its title.**
- **AI Forensics, "TikTok's Research API: Problems Without Explanations", 2025**
  (`arXiv:2506.09746`) — this arc's named receiver, already read in full at session 112.

## 3. The atlas and the dataset register

The atlas of 505 works returns **nothing** on retrievability, availability, link rot, oEmbed,
takedown or research-interface access; its ten hits on "decay" are unrelated works of art (e.g.
*Digital Decay III*, *birth and decay*). The dataset register of 59 sources returns **nothing**
on any of the search terms. Both are recorded as negative results, not as absence of checking.

## 4. What was searched, so the gap in the search is legible

Catalogue term list, applied to the full JSON of every entry, case-insensitive:
`retrievab`, `availability`, `link rot`, `linkrot`, `oembed`, `reference rate`, `baseline rate`,
`reachab`, `dead link`, `persistence`, `decay`, `takedown`, `research api`, `data access`, `dsa`.
**A catalogue search is a search of titles and recorded fields, not of full texts**, and the
paper register carries no abstracts in the index form fetched here. A neighbour whose title and
recorded fields use none of these words would not have been found.
