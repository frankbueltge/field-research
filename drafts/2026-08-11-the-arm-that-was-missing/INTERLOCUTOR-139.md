### Attack that PARTIALLY SUCCEEDED — the blinding comparison is not apples-to-apples

I recomputed both sides directly:
- The original **137/483 (28.4%)** figure (`PREREGISTRATION-137B.md` §4b) was computed using exactly **four** named tokens: `Charge N`, `Finding N`, `BLOCKING`, verdict vocabulary. I reproduced this exactly (137/483).
- `blinding_check_137.py`'s TELLS dict actually has *eight* entries, and two of the un-used ones (`'I recomputed'/'recomputed'`, `'I ran'/'I fetched'`) were **already reader-free in the v2 population** (0 reader hits each) — I confirmed this by running `blinding_check_137.py` directly against `units-137-v2.json`. Applying the *same* "any reader-free tell" rule that `blinding_share_139.py` uses for the new figure to the *old* population gives **173/483 (35.8%)**, not 137/483 — meaning the original headline number was itself an undercount by the practice's own script's own rule, and this undercount survived one full adversarial pass (`INTERLOCUTOR-138.md` Attack 5 explicitly "recomputed from source," reproduced 137/483 exactly, and certified it "real, not inflated" — without checking whether the other four tells in the same dict were also reader-free).

So the comparison actually published — **48.9% vs 28.4%**, a jump the document calls large enough to be "stated here at full size rather than in a footnote" — mixes a complete 7-tell union (new) against an incomplete 4-tell union (old). An apples-to-apples comparison under either fixed rule gives a real but roughly *half-sized* increase (37.6% vs 28.4%, or 48.9% vs the corrected 35.8%), not the ~72%-relative jump the document reports. The direction of the finding ("hand-delimited units are more role-revealing") survives under the corrected comparison — this does not reverse anything — but the stated magnitude is inflated by a real, previously-uncaught methodology inconsistency, and the claim that "no tell could drift between the two measurements" is not accurate: the *tells table* didn't drift, but the *selection rule* applied to it did, silently, across two different sessions. **This is a genuine, checkable finding this pass should have caught and did not.**

## OBLIGATION (b) — THE HOSTILE CRITIC

Strip the apparatus and ask what an outside reader — someone who does not already believe in this practice's own vocabulary of K-conditions, pre-registrations, and convened roles — would see in `DELIMITATION-139.md`.

**What actually happened this session:** four LLM instances, given the same instructions and the same criterion, read ten files each and mostly wrote down the same boundary markers. That is the entire empirical event. Everything else in this document — the pool-size arithmetic, the batch-split rule, the K4″ non-scoring, the extractor comparison, the blinding re-measurement — is bookkeeping *around* that one event, and it is, without exception, bookkeeping the practice does about its own bookkeeping. Six days before the architect's 2026-09-05 reading, the practice has produced its first batch of raw material in nineteen sessions of designing how to produce raw material, and the material is still, by the document's own words, unclassified, rate-less, and un-usable by anyone outside this repository. "Nothing is classified here. No rate exists" is not a modest caveat — it is the entire finding, stated as a disclaimer.

**Is the "four counters agreeing" result actually evidence of anything an outside reader should care about?** No. Four instances of the same kind of reader, given the same rule, converging on the same markers 95% of the time is what you'd expect from *any* well-specified, low-ambiguity text-segmentation task performed by four copies of a similarly-capable process — it is barely more informative than "a deterministic function is deterministic." The document itself, to its credit, says this in the "what this does not establish" section, quoting its own INTERLOCUTOR-134 charge 1 verbatim and admitting nothing here repairs it. But naming the hole is not the same as caring whether the hole matters, and here it obviously does: the entire evidentiary weight of "178 units now exist" rests on four readers being different enough from each other that their agreement means something about the *reports*, not about the *readers*. Nobody in this practice has ever varied that dimension — different prompt framings, different models, an actual disagreeing human — across nineteen sessions of a design explicitly built to answer a hit-rate question. The cheapest possible experiment that would test the charge (give one counter a materially different instruction and see if agreement collapses) has never been run, and it would cost one role-slot.

**The blinding finding is the one place this session did something a critic should respect, and even there it pulls its punch.** 48.9% of units carry a role-revealing token — worse than the machine it's replacing — and the document says so plainly, at length, unhedged, with the sentence "P3 depends on this blinding, and 48.9% is what it now rests on for these units." That is honest. What it is not is *consequential*: the document names the number and stops. It does not ask whether P3 — the single downstream prediction this entire blinding apparatus exists to protect — is now salvageable at all, does not propose a fix, does not flag which units should be excluded from a future blinded read, and (as shown above) doesn't even get the comparison to its own predecessor right. A reader who came to this document because they cared whether this practice's classification pipeline will work would leave with a number and no answer to the question the number was raised to answer.
