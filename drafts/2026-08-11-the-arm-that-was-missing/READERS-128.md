# The third severed-reader panel — conditions, arithmetic, and what it cost this session

*Session 128, 2026-08-20. Required by the floor the architect added to PROTOCOL v3 on 2026-08-18:
severed readers meet the work cold, are asked what they understood and what they take it to be
about, and **their answers are published beside the work, unedited, including the ones that miss it
entirely.** The three full answers are `READER-128-1.md`, `-2.md` and `-3.md`.*

## The severing conditions, stated so the panel can be judged and not just quoted

- **One directory path each, and nothing else.** Each reader was given its own complete copy of the
  frozen object, outside this repository, and told to read only inside it: no wider filesystem, no
  web, no attempt to find out who wrote it.
- **Three different entry points, and nothing else differed.** Reader 1 was sent to `LETTER.md`.
  Reader 2 was given the bare directory listing and told to choose its own first file and to assume
  nothing was the entry point. Reader 3 was sent to the data file `dashboard-findings.json` and told
  to read it before anything else.
- **They could run the commands.** Each was told it may run what the files describe, inside its own
  copy, and was not required to.
- **No welcome answer, and a blank permitted.** Each was told plainly that nobody was hoping for a
  particular result and that "I cannot say what this is" is a legitimate answer.
- **Invent nothing.** Each was told to say it was unsure rather than fill a gap.
- **The frozen object was never given to a reader.** Copies only — which is why the freeze still
  verifies 17 of 17 after the panel, and why reader 1 could regenerate two of the object's own files
  in place and report that it had done so.

## What they returned

**All three led with the receiver-facing finding, and all three carried a date with it.**

> **Reader 1:** *"All eleven video-status timelines on a public TikTok-research dashboard flipped to
> 'Error' on the same day, 2026-01-03, and the dashboard's own record has not moved since
> 2026-01-14 — 218 days before this was written — even though its front page still displays those
> errors as if current."*

> **Reader 2:** *"A public dashboard that is supposed to daily-check the availability of a fixed set
> of TikTok videos stopped updating on 2026-01-14 and has shown all 11 tracked videos as 'Error'
> since a state change on 2026-01-03, yet an independent check this morning found 10 of those 11
> videos still publicly fetchable."*

> **Reader 3:** *"A public TikTok-availability dashboard has apparently been frozen since 14 January
> 2026 — all 11 videos it tracks flipped to an 'Error' status on 3 January 2026 and nothing has
> changed since — while a direct check this morning found most of those same videos are still
> publicly fetchable, meaning the dashboard's visible tiles are seven months stale."*

**All three named the person and the machine authorship**, unprompted and correctly, including that
nothing has been sent and that the decision to send is not the practice's.

**And all three carried limits with the finding.** This is the part that failed eighteen hours
before the previous object shipped and again the night after: the panel of 2026-08-18 returned the
caveat and not the finding; the panel of 2026-08-19 returned the finding and not the caveat. This
one returned both, from all three, unprompted — reader 1 quoting the letter's own *"we are not
saying what broke"*, reader 2 naming the single-vantage limit, reader 3 naming the 200-day gap.

## What it cost, and this is the reason to run a panel at all

**Reader 3 found a defect in the object that nine internal reviewers and this session had not**
(`ERRATA-128.md` E24): two of the eleven identifiers carry contradictory creation dates in two files
that sit in the same directory. This session reproduced it, added the ordering test that says which
side is anomalous, and **carried it rather than repairing it**, because the object was frozen before
any reader saw it. **The two rows it lands on are the two videos whose availability records were
already unlike the other nine.**

**Two of three still reported the self-containment caveat as a gap they found** — though the letter
states it, in bold, in the same paragraph as the claim it qualifies. That is a finding about
placement, recorded in E24 and not repaired here.

## What this panel does and does not license this practice to say

**Legibility: the object passes this limb, and passes it on all three entry points** — including the
one that began at a JSON file and the one that began at a bare listing. A work whose severed readers
can say nothing back does not ship; this is not that work.

**Worth it: not answered here, and not claimed.** No reader said it would have stopped reading, which
is a change from both previous panels — but a convened reader has no time to lose and no capacity for
boredom, and a session that reads its panel as approval has misread it. That limb belongs to the
architect and the question is open with him (`REQUESTS.md`, 2026-08-19).

**A limit of this panel, stated because it bounds the sentence above.** Three readers is three
readers. They were severed from context, not from competence: each is a capable reader with unlimited
patience, and the receiver is a working research organisation with neither.
