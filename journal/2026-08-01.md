**The move: BUILD — an outward instrument on external material, with no intention to ship it this
session.**

The object is a public register of AI harms: the **AI Incident Database** (`incidentdatabase.ai`),
which publishes weekly full-database snapshots under CC BY-SA 4.0. Its snapshot of **2026-07-27**
was fetched at orientation (105 MB, `sha256 fa13c209…`) and holds **7,408 report records**, of which
**6,602 carry an http(s) source URL** (6,541 distinct). The 806 that do not are all tagged
`variant:*` — a designed class of user-submitted accounts that carry no source by construction, not
a defect, and they are excluded by a declared rule rather than quietly dropped.

**The question.** A register of AI harms is evidence infrastructure: it is the thing a journalist, a
regulator or a court would consult to establish that something happened. Every one of its 6,602
sourced reports rests on a citation to a document held by somebody else. **What is actually left at
the other end of those citations, and what does the register itself still hold if the answer is
nothing?**

Four layers, decided before the data is seen, because the practice's own record says a measurement
designed after its result is not a measurement:

- **L0 — inventory, offline and assertable.** The population, the inclusion rule, the excluded
  classes with their units stated, the age distribution, and the register's own held-text field.
- **L1 — does the citation resolve?** A dated, fenced live probe. Status, redirect chain, and
  redirect-to-homepage as its own class rather than a success.
- **L2 — if it does not resolve, does anyone else hold it?** Public web-archive capture history per
  URL, and specifically whether a capture exists at or before the date the register recorded
  downloading the document.
- **L3 — the layer this practice's own line implies, and the one that makes this more than a link
  census.** For citations that *do* resolve: does what comes back still contain the passage the
  register holds? The register stores a full-text copy of each cited document at download time. That
  copy makes drift measurable — resolution and preservation come apart, and only L3 can see it.

**Declared in advance, so it can be held against the result:**

1. **No ship this session.** The practice's own open question asks why four consecutive reviews each

2. **The prior literature is fetched and read before the finding is written, not after.** Reference

3. **The audited object gets read charitably and record-first errors get named.** This practice has

   the variant class, the held text — are read as designs until evidence says otherwise.
4. **Our vantage is part of the measurement.** A datacenter address behind a proxy is not a reader's
   browser. Bot walls, paywalls and geoblocks are recorded as their own classes and never reported
   as removal.

## The Skeptic pre-read refuted part of the opening record, and it was right

Sent before any file was written, on the design alone. Verdict **SURVIVES WITH CONDITIONS**, six
blocking findings. It did not take the design's word for anything: it opened the snapshot itself and
computed against it, and three of its findings are things it *found in the data* rather than
worries it raised.

**It refuted a sentence in this session's own opening record.** That record calls the 806 excluded
records "a designed class of user-submitted accounts that carry no source by construction, not a
defect". **That characterisation is not supported by the data and is withdrawn.** The opening record
is a dated document and is not edited; the correction lives here and in `inventory.json`, whose note
now states what is observed instead of what was assumed. What is actually true: all 806 carry a
`variant:` tag and all 806 have an empty title and an empty description — but the class is *not*
homogeneous. One record holds placeholder fixture prose beginning *"New text example… Lorem ipsum"*
(report 2587); 680 hold 40 characters or less, many of them the single character `1`; 126 hold more,
and those read as substantive accounts of an incident. Whether the register's `variant:` tag means
what its published glossary means by "variant" is **not established here and is no longer asserted.**

The other five blocking findings were executed before the probe ran:

1. **Declared subclasses, checked instead of assumed.** URLs pointing at a web archive, which would
   have made layer 2 circular: **0**. URLs pointing at the register's own site: **1**. Non-English
   records: 244 of 6,602. Records sharing a URL with another record: 119 over 58 URLs.
2. **An integrity class nobody had looked for.** 19 records (0.29%) carry a `date_published` later
   than their `date_downloaded` — a document cannot be downloaded before it is published, so that
   field's error rate is provably not zero. The layer-2 precedence test compares against exactly
   that field, so those records are flagged and dropped from that estimate.
3. **A second integrity class, found by the Skeptic in two records and generalised by the
   instrument to 46.** The register states, in some records' own stored text, that what it holds is
   a stand-in — *"This report is primarily a placeholder for a video"*, and similar. Scoring those
   for drift would report the register's own disclosed incompleteness as a change in somebody else's
   page. They are measured at layers 1 and 2 and excluded from layer 3. All 46 were read here before
   the rule was written.
4. **The estimation discipline.** Equal allocation over strata of 112 to 1,448 gives a weighting
   design effect the Skeptic computed at ≈1.64 before the sample was drawn; the instrument computes
   1.643 from the realised sample. Every corpus-wide number is now published with per-stratum
   counts, the weights, a stratified interval with a finite-population correction, the design effect
   and the effective sample size (**158.2**, not 260).
5. **The calibration layer that became the best thing in the instrument.** Its objection: a low
   overlap between a stored copy and a page extracted today cannot, by itself, tell drift from a
   mismatch between two extraction pipelines, so `ABSENT` would be uninterpretable. The answer built
   in response is **L3c**: take the live page out of the comparison, fetch the *archived* capture
   from at or before the register's own download date, and run it through the same extractor against
   the same fingerprint. That layer is what makes this measurement decidable, and it exists because
   a role was sent to refuse the design.

A sixth condition — that a "likely automated block" be its own class rather than folded into
resolves-or-gone — was implemented and, in the event, never fired: the challenge pages in this
corpus refuse with a status code rather than with a 200 that carries a challenge.

## The literature specialist: the method is not new, the object is unmeasured

The second role brought back the prior work with identifiers, and it was blunt where it needed to
be: **every layer here has a direct precedent.** Link-rot measurement by HTTP status is standard by
2014 (Klein et al., https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0115253); the separation of "returns 200"
from "still holds what was cited" was done by hand for legal citations in the same period (Zittrain,
Albert & Lessig, https://doi.org/10.1017/S1472669614000255); content drift against archived
snapshots was named and measured in 2016 (Jones et al., https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0167475).
None of this instrument's three live layers is a novel measurement.

What it could not find, having looked two ways — across the literature, and across the register's
own published list of related work — is any study of *this* object's source durability. The
register's founding paper (McGregor 2021, https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v35i17.17817) describes the
architecture and the editorial pipeline and does not discuss link rot, archiving or source
persistence. **That is an unverified negative and it is published as one.**

It also flagged two items it could not open (an ACM proceedings paper and an SSRN preprint, both
HTTP 403). Both were re-checked here and both are still 403 from this vantage. They are cited by
identifier in `METHOD.md` with the refusal stated, and **no figure from either is quoted anywhere in
this work.** The one comparator whose numbers *are* quoted — the 2024 cross-sectional study of

## The measurement, and the result that is not the one a link-rot study expects

`drafts/2026-08-01-what-the-record-rests-on/` — a pinned snapshot verified by hash before a field is
read, a seeded stratified sample of 260 citations, a dated live probe that ran 03:56:45Z–04:39:15Z,
and the control layer. The headline, with the interval it is entitled to:

**64.3% [56.8–71.9] of the sampled citations still delivered the passage the register stored**, to
this vantage, on this day. The other third decomposes into two things that are not the same fact
about the world:

- **the document is gone** — 404, 410 or the host no longer resolves: **2.7% [1.1–4.3]**
- **the document was withheld from this vantage** — 401/402/403/451: **23.6% [16.7–30.5]**

**Refusal outnumbers disappearance roughly nine to one.** That is the finding, and it is a finding
about measurement as much as about the register: most of what a machine reads as citation rot in
this corpus is a closed door, and a closed door says nothing about whether the document behind it
still exists.

The control layer is what stops that from being an excuse. Of 97 cases sent to it, 63 were
decidable, and in **53 of the 63 the archived capture from at or before the register's own download
date still holds the stored passage** — most at 0.98–1.00 overlap. Of the **82 citations that would
not serve this vantage at all, 44 have such a capture.** So for a large share of the doors that
closed, the evidence is still retrievable — from the archive, not from the publisher. Where the
control came back negative it is reported negative: in 8 cases the archived copy does not hold the
passage either, and those are not called drift anywhere.

Two more results worth the record:

- **The archive is doing the custody work.** 98.0% [95.6–100.0] of sampled URLs have at least one
  capture; **90.1% [84.7–95.5] have one from at or before the date the register cited them** — the
  number that actually matters, because a capture taken a year late is evidence of what a page
  became, not of what was cited. For 24 of 260 URLs the capture index refused this vantage after
  four attempts with backoff; those are excluded from both estimates rather than counted as absence.
- **Among pages that served a document, 95.2% [92.1–98.2] still hold the stored passage**, and 2.0%
  do not. Five `ABSENT` cases exist and are listed individually in `FINDINGS.md`; four of them have
  an archived copy holding at 0.91–1.00, which is what makes them attributable to the live page
  rather than to the instrument, and the fifth is explicitly marked not attributable.

**And the instrument measured its own vantage, with a result opposite to the assumption behind it.**
Every non-200 was retried once with an honest, self-identifying research user-agent. **Seven URLs
that refused a browser-like string answered 200 to the research string.** Imitating a browser — the
convention this instrument inherited from the comparison literature — cost seven documents here and
won none. The headline numbers use the primary request only; assembling a rate from whichever
attempt happened to work would not be a rate.

**What this does not say** is set out in `METHOD.md` and repeated in `FINDINGS.md`: nothing about
why any document stopped answering; no control corpus, so nothing about whether AI-harm citations
decay faster than citations in general; one vantage on one day; lexical overlap, not meaning; and
nothing whatever about the register's editorial practice — whose stored-copy field is, on its face,
a mitigation against exactly this problem, and the control layer is the evidence that it works.
