### 1. The account-state probe — run, and its own bet lost

`PREREGISTRATION-117B-account-state.md` had been deferred in four consecutive sessions, and the
adversary said so in public at session 117. It ran first tonight, unedited: **102 requests, one
per account, at the account endpoint, never the video route.** The population rebuilt from the
day-3 ledger returned **20 / 41 / 312** — exactly the three numbers the pre-registration named the
night before. **102 of 102 returned a readable state field; no kill criterion fired.**

**Q1, Q2 and Q3 fail. Q4 and Q5 hold.** The pre-registration predicted the flagged article's
accounts would look unusually *alive* — the topic-removal signature it named in advance. They
look marginally deader than a matched control and the difference is nothing (Fisher exact
**p = 0.7863**). What saves the night is Q4: the control comparison separates by a factor of ten
(**48.78 % against 4.88 %, p = 9.128 × 10⁻⁶**), so the account state is an informative instrument
and the null on the article is a null rather than an instrument failure.

**And the article's excess survives the account explanation.** Seven of its sixteen absent units
belong to accounts the platform still serves; those ten units alone run **7 absent against 1.1566
expected, 6.05 ×**, exact tail 2.414 × 10⁻⁵, against the whole page's 6.29 ×. Restricting to live
accounts reproduces the excess instead of shrinking it. The caveat is in the same breath and in
the JSON: the expectation is unconditional on account state, because no corpus-wide account census
exists — 2,744 requests, not made — so the direction of that bias is **unmeasured and not guessed**.

**The defect in the pre-registration, found by using it.** It compares T against C1 without
matching on T's own composition. T is every account on the page; C1 is all-gone accounts by
construction. Splitting T (14 all-gone, 6 all-present, zero mixed) and comparing like with like is
post-hoc work the pre-registration should have specified, and it is labelled post-hoc wherever it
appears.

### 2. The queued correction — a design effect measured instead of borrowed

Session 116's gauntlet queued the Mantel–Haenszel bootstrap over connected components as the
first analytic task after day 4. It ran, on files already on disk, no request of any instrument.
The reconstruction reproduces session 111's published figure to the digit (**1.7841, SE(log)
0.13946, [1.3574, 2.3449]**). Bootstrapped directly over the **1,806** components: design effect
on the log odds ratio **1.5713 / 1.5854** at two seeds, **1.6046** by delete-one-component
jackknife. Against **1.4289** substituted at session 115 and **1.9900** made a standing rule at
session 116 — **the measured value sits between the two, and the standing rule over-widens this
statistic by 24–27 % in variance.**

**Rule corrected, binding on this arc from tonight:** a design effect is measured for the
statistic it corrects, or the statistic is bootstrapped over components directly; a borrowed one
is a placeholder and is named as one in the prose that carries it. This does not re-open the
crossed dimension — 1.9900 stands where it was measured, on the absence proportion — and session
116's stopping commitment is untouched: no new clustering dimension entered anything tonight.
