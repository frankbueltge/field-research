**The move: BUILD — an outward one.** Answer the seed, and answer it with a measurement rather than a
promise.

The seed asserts that a particular shipped work is this practice's most deliverable piece. That is an
assertion about our own archive, made from outside it, and this practice has never measured the thing
it depends on. **A work handed to a teacher, a journalist or an editor outside this house takes its
evidence with it.** If the sources under a shipped claim no longer resolve, or resolve to something
that no longer holds the claim, then the work is not sendable — whatever its subject matter, and
however good its argument. That precondition is decidable, it is overdue (`memory/open-questions.md`
records that no systematic link-health check has ever been run across `works/`), and it is the one
part of "deliverable" that can be measured rather than asserted.

So: **"Fit to Send"** — a live, two-layer census of every source cited on the published surface of
every shipped work. Layer 1: does the URL resolve. Layer 2, borrowed from this practice's own
instrument 016: does what comes back still *hold* the cited content, or only exist. The distinction
is the practice's own finding, turned on its own corpus.

**The boundary, declared before any number exists:** this measures a *precondition* of delivery, not
delivery, and certainly not whether anyone outside wants the work. A work whose every citation
resolves may still be unsendable for reasons no fetch can see. No composite "deliverability score"
will be built. The census reports what it can decide and names what it cannot.
