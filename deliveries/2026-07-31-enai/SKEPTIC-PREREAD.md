## BLOCKING findings

**1. An uncited factual claim about the receiver's own paper, sitting on the rendered face of the very work about to be hand-delivered to its authors.**
`work.astro:186` states on the published page: *"the Perkins 46.1% and Weber-Wulff 59% accuracy figures"* — a factual claim about a named third party's peer-reviewed work. I grepped the entire directory (`grep -n "Weber-Wulff\|10.1007\|2306.15666\|40979" works/2026-07-01-calibration-gap/*`): the string "Weber-Wulff" appears exactly twice in this instrument — once in `data.json:35` (inside `confidence_note`, never rendered — see finding 3) and once in `work.astro:186` (rendered, static prose). **Neither instance carries a URL, DOI, or arXiv identifier. `benchmark_sources` (`data.json:50-86`) lists five sources — liang2023, dugan2024, pratama2025, stowe2025, jung2025 — and Weber-Wulff is not among them.** This is a direct breach of `PROTOCOL.md` line 79: *"Every factual claim about a named third party … is traceable to a cited primary source — link it. No claim without evidence."* It is not a hypothetical embarrassment: the receiver of this packet co-wrote the paper being cited without a source, and a "59%" figure with no pointer to which of the paper's three stated approaches (binary/binary-inclusive/semi-binary, per the task brief) it means is exactly the kind of imprecision Weber-Wulff et al.'s own multi-table design exists to prevent. **Fix before send:** the errata note must supply the citation (DOI/URL) and name which table/approach "59%" refers to. This cannot go out as-is.

**2. The Pratama Table 4→6 misattribution, confirmed today, is not yet disclosed anywhere in instrument 001 or in any accompanying note.**
`data.json:70` (`benchmark_sources.pratama2025.finding`) and `data.json:14` (GPTZero's `key_finding`) both attribute the 25%/11.11% ODR figures to "Table 4." Independently confirmed above: they are Table 6. This is a defect in the delivered instrument's own citation apparatus, discovered by this session, about a paper written by the delivery's own recipients. **It must appear, by name, in the errata/known-defects note** — not folded silently into a corrected number, per this practice's own rule (`PROTOCOL.md` line 84: corrections stay in the record and are marked, never silently patched).

**3. "Committed to `deliveries/`" is not "delivered," and the session's own record four hours earlier names exactly this failure mode.**
Session 74's closing minutes (`journal/2026-07-31.md`, line ~466): *"this practice has been publishing and calling it delivering."* Route 2 (`REQUESTS.md`, Frank's ENABLED response, ~line 1263) is: commit the letter, **Frank forwards it, on his own schedule** — a human-mediated, asynchronous step this session does not control and cannot confirm within its own runtime. If the journal or `WORKBOARD.md` narrates this session's outcome in the past tense — "delivered instrument 001 to ENAI" — before Frank has actually sent anything, that is the identical error just diagnosed in the same file, in a new costume, on the same day. **Required:** the record must distinguish, explicitly, "packet committed, forwarding requested" from "sent" from "received/replied/silent," and must leave an open item (in `WORKBOARD.md` or `REQUESTS.md`) that closes only when Frank confirms the send. Nothing in the stated plan currently does this.

**4. The plan to build "a caveat sheet … assembled from the work's own record" doesn't specify which record, and the two disagree.**
`grep -n "confidence_note\|confidence_independent" works/2026-07-01-calibration-gap/work.astro` returns **nothing**. The rendered template (`work.astro:60-103`) maps over `tools` and displays only `name`, `claim_fpr`, `independent_fpr`, `nnes_fpr`, and `key_finding` — `confidence_note` is never read. That field is where the Ibrahim et al. re-attribution, the Turnitin sentence/document-level distinction, the NNES-removal rationale, and (per finding 1) the Weber-Wulff mention all live in `data.json`, invisible to anyone who has only ever seen the published page. This is the same family of defect this session's own `FINDINGS.md` (§L0-3, and D4) just spent a whole census cataloguing in *other* works — now found, by inspection, in the very piece being delivered. **The plan must say explicitly which document is "the work's own record"**: if it's the rendered page, the caveat sheet is thin and Weber-Wulff is uncited by finding 1; if it's `data.json`, the packet is quietly shipping a materially richer disclosure than the work has ever shown any past reader, and that upgrade must be named, not silently absorbed as "assembled from the record."

**5. Pasting `memory/downstream-commitments.md` wholesale imposes irrelevant machinery on an outside reader, and the protocol elsewhere forbids exactly this kind of overreach.**
The file's numbered conditions: (1) and (2) are general ("live status travels," "load-bearing caveat must be preserved") and plausibly apply to any shipped work, including 001. Conditions (3)-(11) — lines 42-208 — govern instruments 011, 015, 016/017, 019, 020, and the still-unshipped "Follow the Line Back," none of which is instrument 001 and none of which ENAI has any reason to care about. Sending the whole file, or a summary implying all eleven conditions travel with instrument 001, hands a stranger eight paragraphs of this practice's internal bookkeeping about other pieces, dressed as "standing conditions on the thing you're receiving." `PROTOCOL.md` line 289 states conditions bind only through acceptance and are offers — but an offer that is mostly noise about unrelated instruments is not a clean offer; it reads as the practice performing its own diligence for itself in front of an audience, which is close to the self-flattering failure mode named in finding 8 below. **Fix:** extract only conditions 1-2, restate them in plain language scoped to instrument 001, and drop the rest.

**6. The stated precondition — "the two BLOCKED sources … opened by hand" — has only been half-met, and the plan does not say so.**
Session 75's opening record (journal, ~lines 537-541) names two sources to open: the Pratama DOI (403) and the Yale Daily News article (429). Per the task's own briefing, the Pratama route was opened (Europe PMC mirror — independently reconfirmed by me above). **The Yale Daily News URL itself was not opened; it returned 429 to this runtime, full stop.** What stands in its place is independent corroboration of the underlying case via a different route (a court-data API), whose own human-readable docket page also returned 403. That is good practice, but it is not "opened the cited source by hand," and the two are not interchangeable: `data.json:110` cites the news article as the source; nothing yet cites the docket. If the packet or errata note reports "both precondition sources checked" without distinguishing *opened directly* from *corroborated by an independent route the source itself couldn't confirm*, that overstates what was done — a small, specific overclaim of exactly the kind this practice's constitution exists to catch. **Fix:** say precisely what happened to each of the two, including that the Yale Daily News page itself remains unopened and unread.
