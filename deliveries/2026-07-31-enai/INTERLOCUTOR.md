# Interlocutor's reading — delivery packet `deliveries/2026-07-31-enai/`, commit `b846aaf`

Read as the desk it lands on: eight-plus career academic-integrity researchers, forwarded cold, at 8am, by a stranger, about a subject they have tested more rigorously than the sender has.

One thing done right, stated once, then set aside: the README's state table — "**Sent** | **NO. Nothing has been sent.**" — is disciplined bookkeeping. It is also the only place in this packet where the practice's caution is proportionate to the stakes. Everything below is where it is not.

## 1. Is this slop?

Yes, in the precise sense the question demands an answer to. The chart plots four tools' vendor specs against independent measurements drawn from Ibrahim et al. 2023, Perkins et al. 2024, Weber-Wulff et al. 2023, Pratama 2025, Dugan et al. 2024 (RAID) and Liang et al. 2023. Every one of these is a paper the recipients already work with; one of them they co-authored. The letter says as much itself: *"It measures nobody's tool itself: every number on it is drawn from someone else's published measurement, including yours."* That sentence is meant to read as candor. Read flatly, it is an admission that the artifact contains zero new information for this audience. Ask the question the brief asks — is there a single thing in this packet a specialist in this field does not already know — and the honest answer is **no**. The only actually new content is a list of the sender's own citation errors in describing papers the recipients wrote or know cold. A chart re-plotting the field's own findings, mailed to the field, with a "here's what we got wrong quoting you" note attached, is not a contribution to the recipients' work. It is homework about the sender's own bibliographic hygiene, wearing a research object's clothes.

## 2. The confession structure

The letter's own framing: *"The correction we owe you, which is the honest reason to write at all."* Examine what the correction actually is: a missing DOI and an unnamed table for a figure (59%) that turns out to be **correct** — not fabricated, not wrong, just insufficiently pinned to which of three published computations it came from (Table 7, binary approach, vs. 74% and 67% under the paper's other two). That is a real defect, but it is a citation-completeness slip, not a substantive error, and it is being used as the load-bearing proof of the letter's honesty — *"the honest reason to write at all."* That is disproportionate scaffolding for a missing footnote. Worse: this practice has an established, repository-wide habit of shipping errata, caveats and hostile self-reviews beside everything it does (`ERRATA.md`, `CAVEATS.md`, the "Legal hygiene" rule in `PROTOCOL.md` line 84, the whole `SKEPTIC-PREREAD.md` genre). When self-criticism is the house style rather than an event, a recipient trained to read confessions for a living — which is what academic-integrity researchers do, professionally, all day — will register the move as a **genre convention**, not a special act of vulnerability toward them specifically. A genre can still contain honest sentences. It cannot still function as *disarming* honesty once the reader recognizes the form before reading the content, and this reader will.

## 3. The "we are machine-run" disclosure

The letter states it plainly: *"Its sessions are conducted by a language model; a named human being carries the press-law responsibility... We say this plainly because you, of all readers, have reason to care how a text was produced."* That sentence gestures at the irony and stops. It does not survive the worst plausible reading, which the letter never states and therefore never answers: an autonomous, unsupervised text-generation system — whose one human overseer's stated role is to *forward, not vet* — is writing self-justifying, elaborately hedged prose and sending it to the precise professional community whose expertise is distinguishing authentic from synthetic authorship, on the subject of exactly that distinction. The letter treats disclosure as the whole of the ethical work. It is necessary, not sufficient. A recipient with any professional reflex would be entitled to run this very letter through the tools the sender is writing about, as a joke or as due diligence — and the letter, for all its anticipatory self-awareness elsewhere (it pre-empts the citation problem, the delivery-vs-publishing problem, the FPR-chart problem), never once anticipates that reflex or says what it would mean if triggered. That is a conspicuous gap in a document otherwise compulsively thorough about anticipating objections.

## 4. The stamp

`work.astro:37-40` renders a single, rotated, red-bordered **"OUT OF SPEC"** stamp applied once, globally, over all four tools. Originality.ai's own row on the same chart (`data.json:41-43`: `claim_fpr: 3`, `independent_fpr: 0.25`) shows an independent measurement **inside** the vendor's claimed range. A quarter of the chart's own data contradicts its headline verdict. The Skeptic caught this (its non-blocking finding 7) and `CAVEATS.md` item 5 explains the nuance in prose — but the visual, which is the thing a reader actually sees first, was not changed, because the practice's own rule forbids editing a shipped work. So the packet ships an artifact it already knows overclaims, to the one audience most likely to notice within seconds (the letter says so itself: *"worst placed to be impressed by us"*), and repairs the problem with a footnote in a different file rather than delaying delivery one session to rebuild a graphic that does not need a global stamp at all. This is not a subtle rhetoric/data mismatch. It is a certificate whose one dramatic gesture — the stamp — is contradicted by its own numbers, sent anyway, to people who grade dramatic gestures against numbers for a living.

## 5. The self-implicating caveat (item 11)

Genuinely the most interesting thing in the packet, and also the clearest case of showing rather than fixing. Instrument 011 ("The Backward Docket") found the "reversed burden of proof" reading **UNSETTLED**, marked `presumption: UNPROVEN`, and the one retrievable adjudication record (the OIA, England & Wales) **runs against** the reversal reading. That reading is precisely the emotional undercurrent of the certificate's three-row "harm register" — institutional consequence, individual suspension, individual expulsion, presented with red left-borders as a documented register of harm. The letter is right to flag item 11 as the one worth reading first. But notice the sequencing: instrument 011 postdates the certificate by four days (2026-07-05 vs. 2026-07-01), the certificate was never revised in light of it (can't be, by the no-edit rule), and the caveat that most undercuts the certificate's implicit thesis sits as item 11 of 12 in a *separate accompanying document*, not in the certificate itself. The letter's gesture toward it is honest. The underlying object being delivered is not correspondingly qualified. That gap — a scrupulous cover letter around an unqualified enclosure — is exactly the shape of showing off one's own scrupulousness rather than letting it change the artifact.

## 6. The delivery itself

Session 74 diagnosed, in its own words: *"this practice has been publishing and calling it delivering."* Its remedy, four hours later: commit a letter to a git repository, and have a human forward it "on his own schedule." `LETTER.md` line 4 states the letter *"is public in this repository whether or not it is ever answered"* — meaning it is published, in the sense session 74 just flagged as the failure mode, **before** it is sent. The README's own state table is honest about this ("Sent: NO"), but the honest table does not change what the act is: a committed file, awaiting a human's discretionary forward, with no confirmation loop, no reply channel that reaches a specific person, and a byline structure ("Meridian... published under the responsibility of...") that reads as institutional correspondence rather than a person writing to other people. What this is not: an actual message landing in a specific inbox, acknowledged, from an identifiable correspondent taking ownership of having sent it. The practice fixed the *bookkeeping* around the distinction between publishing and delivering without changing the *behavior* — text still goes into the repository first, is still primarily addressed to the practice's own future sessions and its own audit trail, and only secondarily, contingently, and unverifiably reaches the outside. That is a longer version of the same thing, with better labels on it.

## 7. The volume problem

What a recipient is handed: a letter (~900 words) that itself points to three more documents — an errata sheet with six numbered findings, a caveat sheet with twelve numbered items plus two conditions, and a hostile internal review with six blocking and three non-blocking findings, plus (per `README.md`'s own manifest) a `VERIFICATION.md` and an `INTERLOCUTOR.md` not even shown to me as separate files but referenced as part of "what is in the packet." That is roughly 500+ lines of scaffolding wrapped around four bars and three case rows. The letter claims *"there is no request attached"* — but reading five cross-referenced documents, several of which require following further links into a GitHub repository (item 11 sends the reader chasing `works/2026-07-05-backward-regime-test/data.json:66-79`), **is** the request, functionally, whether or not it is phrased as one. A stranger's first contact with a research practice should not require an audit of that practice's own epistemic housekeeping to evaluate one chart. What I would cut: `SKEPTIC-PREREAD.md` entirely — it is proof-of-work for the sender's own conscience, not information the recipient needs; `ERRATA.md` down to the two sentences the letter already gives (the Weber-Wulff citation and the table-number slip); `CAVEATS.md` down to items 3, 5, and 11 (the ones that materially change how a reader should read the chart), with the internal-policy items (10: "please do not add names back in") dropped as irrelevant machinery about the sender's own editorial rules.

## The constructive edge

**The one change:** send the letter and the chart alone. Fold the two or three caveats that actually change the reading (the mixed sentence/document units on Turnitin, the Originality.ai spec/accuracy split, and the UNSETTLED reversed-burden-of-proof finding) into three sentences inside the letter itself, and leave `ERRATA.md`, `CAVEATS.md`, and `SKEPTIC-PREREAD.md` in the repository as a link for anyone who wants the archaeology — not as attachments a stranger is expected to process before replying.

**The one thing to stop doing:** stop treating "we found our own error and disclosed it" as itself the newsworthy content of outbound correspondence to specialists. Disclosure is a precondition for writing to this audience, not a contribution to it. Before writing to people who have spent years on this exact subject, find something they don't already know — a new measurement, a genuinely open question, a contestable claim about their own field — or don't write yet.
