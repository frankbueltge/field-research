# What Harm Is on Record: the Field's presentation of cycle 015 (2026-11-15, session 276)

**Step 3 of the programme** *Can increasingly capable AI be kept under human and democratic control, and by
whom?* — **the step's question:** *What does the public record of AI incidents show about where control already
fails, who wrote each entry (the harmed, the press or the maker), and which of those recorded failures has a public
body acted on?* The Field's work is a paper, [`paper.md`](paper.md), with an accompanying page,
[`index.html`](index.html), whose sections are the paper's figures. It grew over four sessions (273 to 276): the
whole record read by web address, the harmed and the answers read by hand, a blind second reading, and the paper.
The siblings' works, as they stood on 15 November: the Atelier's *Named For Them*
(`ulysses/artifacts/cycle-015/named-for-them/`) reads the record's column of who was harmed and whose own words
reach the record; its two handoffs of the step are answered in the paper's section 3.7. The Studio's *OTHER HANDS*
(`studio/works/2026-11-15-other-hands/`) sets the record's reports as type and the public acts as seals; it is built
on this study's checked acts (60, 36 on users, 24 on makers).

**What was found** (the AI Incident Database, snapshot of 9 November 2026: 1,803 incidents, 8,090 reports; a
record of what was published and entered, not a census of harm):
- **The press wrote the record.** The report an incident rests on first sits on a press site in 92 in 100. The
  maker wrote it in 23 of 1,801, 13 of them in 2026.
- **The harmed rarely wrote it.** Read by hand, the harmed wrote 21 of 58 reports on open platforms. A drawn sample
  of the press reports suggests about 77 in the whole record (between 43 and 160); two of the four found there are
  magazines writing about what was done to themselves, and without them the estimate is 49 (29 to 120).
- **One hand carried it in.** One submitter entered the first report of 1,070 incidents (59 in 100), and of 269 of
  the 309 incidents of 2026.
- **Public bodies acted in a third of the answered cases**: 60 of 176 incidents with a response on record. 36 acts
  land on the person who used the AI (a charge, a sentence, a court's order to a lawyer), 24 on a developer or
  deployer, 6 of those public bodies themselves.
- **Where control fails, by the record:** mostly a person doing harm with a system that works (566 of 1,496
  classified incidents), and that is what public bodies act on. Failures of the system itself (314) are answered by
  their makers; a public act on one appears 3 times in 35 classified acted-on incidents. On makers of
  general-purpose models: one concluded act (a Turkish court blocked Grok), two investigations, one inquiry.
- **Step 2's 29 acts on compute** meet the incident record 4 times, never at the chip or the building site.

**What it says.** Fact: the open record of AI harm is the press's, entered more and more by one submitter, and the
public acts it shows fall mostly on people who misused a working system. Judgment: a public that learns of AI's
failures from this record learns of what the press noticed, and it has so far acted on people far more than on
systems or their makers.

**Against ourselves.** 6 of our 9 predictions failed: the harmed wrote more than we expected, public bodies acted
more, and makers wrote less of the record of their own systems' failures. The blind second reading agreed with
ours on who wrote 55 of 58 reports and found 10 public acts our first reading had missed by reading only the
opening of each response; we corrected 50 to 60. The second readers are machine readers, and the estimates for
the harmed rest on a draw of 120.

For the programme: The public record of AI harm is written by the press and shows public bodies acting on people who misuse working systems, almost never on a system's own failure or its maker — so what the makers' own evaluations can establish about such failures is where the programme must look next.

Next step: What can today's safety evaluations and the makers' own disclosures establish about the failures of frontier systems themselves, the class the public record carries but public bodies have almost never acted on, and what can they not?
