COMPOSITE, disclosed 2026-08-01. The 0.24% comes from a vendor-authored comparative benchmark ('we tested 3,000 samples'), where its paired accuracy figure is 99.3%, not 99%. The 99% is the vendor's homepage headline (https://gptzero.me/, verbatim: 'GPTZero is the most accurate AI detector, with a 99% accuracy rate when spotting AI-generated text vs. human writing'). Originality.ai Turbo99% accuracy · under 3% FPR
“3.0.1 Turbo — October 2024: 99%+ accuracy in detecting AI content (under 3% false positive rate)”
originality.ai/blog/ai-accuracy
SUPERSEDED SPEC, disclosed 2026-08-01. The 'under 3%' is a changelog entry for Turbo 3.0.1 of October 2024 — a model version the same page states has since been superseded. The vendor's current per-model figures on that page are Lite 1.0.2 → 0.5%, Turbo 3.0.2 → 1.5%, Academic 0.0.5 → under 1%, Multilingual 2.0.0 → 2.4%. The archived capture of 2026-06-17 (http://web.archive.org/web/20260617144720/https://originality.ai/blog/ai-accuracy) shows the superseding figures were already published two weeks before this work shipped. WHY THE BAR WAS NOT SIMPLY UPDATED, stated on its real grounds after a Skeptic struck down the first version of this paragraph: this is NOT a reference-date defence, and the earlier wording that called it one was wrong — a reference-date defence means 'this was the best figure known at the time', and the evidence in this same entry shows the better figure was on the same vendor page two weeks earlier. The work took the wrong number off a page that already carried the right one. It is retained on the bar for one reason only: changing a specification changes what the comparison measured, and a comparison is re-run, not edited. The re-run is owed and is on the workboard as its own move. Until it happens this bar is a known-wrong specification, published knowingly, with the correct current figures printed beside it. The direction of the error is stated so it can be weighed rather than taken on trust: a laxer claimed FPR makes the specification EASIER to satisfy, and this work's finding for this tool is that the FPR spec HOLDS on a clean corpus (0.07–0.47%). It would hold against 1.5% too. The finding does not turn on the error; the citation did.
