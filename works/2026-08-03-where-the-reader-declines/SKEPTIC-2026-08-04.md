# Skeptic — the second-reader correction to instrument 021's population split

**Run:** 2026-08-04, session 88, against `drafts/2026-08-04-second-reader-021/` and the correction
it produced in `works/2026-08-03-where-the-reader-declines/`, at HEAD `5eefb02`.

**Core claim under attack:** instrument 021's published population split (39 of 60) is not
reproduced by two independent blind readers, who each returned 23, agreeing with each other at
κ = 0.96 and with the published split at 0.54/0.70, never adding a case the original excluded —
so "32 of 39 (82 %)" does not survive, while the underlying finding (the machine over-uses
`contextualizes` and never declines) survives and strengthens.

**Verdict: SURVIVES WITH CONDITIONS.** The load-bearing part of the claim — the 39 does not
reproduce, independent readers converge near 23, all 21 exclusions are unanimous — is real. I
re-ran the pipeline independently and it reproduces exactly. What does **not** survive at the
strength stated is the interpretive layer on top of that number: "reproduces" is true only in a
narrow, already-disclosed sense; "strictly more inclusive, in one direction" is stated more
strongly than the design can license; "gets stronger" / "roughly doubles" is the more dramatic of
two pre-registered branches, and the less dramatic, equally pre-registered branch is statistically
indistinguishable from the published figure; and the one supporting quantification (Fisher
p = 0.039) could not be independently reproduced from anything committed to the repository.

All computation below is in `/tmp/claude-0/skeptic/` (`analyze.py`, `ratio_sensitivity.py`,
`verify_fisher.py` / `verify_fisher2.py` / `verify_fisher3.py`, `direction_null.py`). Nothing was
fixed; this is the attack, not the repair.

## S1 (attack A) — independence vs. prompt determinism

**What I did.** Re-derived R1×R2 κ from the raw reader files (matches `results.json`'s 0.9602
exactly). Then split the 60 cases into the 21 the original excluded ("easy zone" — the two readers
have almost zero variance there: R1 touches it once with an UNDECIDABLE, R2 never leaves OUT) and
the 39 the original included ("contested zone" — the actual test of whether the *judgement*, not
just the obvious cases, reproduces). Recomputed κ and raw agreement on the contested zone alone.
Checked whether any model/temperature/version metadata is recorded for either reader.

**What I found.** Restricted to the 39 contested cases, R1×R2 raw agreement is 32/39 = 82.1 % and
κ = 0.919 — barely below the whole-set 0.960. **The high agreement is not an artefact of 21 trivial
shared exclusions**; it holds up inside the zone that was actually in dispute. That defeats the
crudest version of this attack (that κ = 0.96 is mostly "both readers correctly ignore the obvious
21"). But nothing in the design discriminates a second, subtler version: no file anywhere records
which model, version, or temperature produced R1 or R2, so there is no way to check whether they
are two draws from the same distribution (which would explain high agreement as *sampling
consistency of one function*, not independent judgement) or two genuinely different capabilities.
`RULE.md` §4 and §10 already say, in its own words, that both readers "are convened roles of this
practice on an efficient model tier... They are not the outside," and that "a correlated error
between them would be invisible to this design." That is the correct caveat, stated in advance —
but the work's own headline sentence ("the population judgement is not inherently unstable. It
reproduces.") reads past it.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY SUCCEEDS. NOT BLOCKING** — the limitation is real but is already disclosed,
not smuggled in. What the attack earns is a demand that "reproduces" be read exactly as narrowly as
§10 already frames it, and that the headline sentence not be quoted on its own without that frame.

## S2 (attack B) — a different, narrower question

**What I did.** Compared the criterion text quoted verbatim in `RULE.md` §3 against
`build_data.py`'s docstring and the `EXCLUDED` dict's own header comment, to check whether the
readers were shown the same criterion, over the same source material, as the original builder used.
Checked whether the exact prompt text sent to R1/R2 is committed anywhere (it is not — `RULE.md` is
a description, not a transcript).

**What I found.** The strong form of this attack fails: the criterion sentence
("does this source's own system do research...") is quoted character-for-character from
`build_data.py`, and both `POPULATION`'s and `EXCLUDED`'s header comments confirm the original
judgement was itself made "from title and excerpt" — the same two fields the readers saw. This is a
genuine like-for-like comparison at the level of the criterion and the source text.

A narrower form survives, though. Two things:

1. **`UNDECIDABLE` was real, disclosed, and asymmetric between readers**, and its effect is larger
   than the correction foregrounds. R2 used it 8 times against R1's 3 — nearly half of R2's total
   departure from the published split (16 cases) landed on the hedge rather than a firm OUT. This
   is not a hidden problem (`DEVIATIONS.md` D1 computes both branches), but see S4: under the branch
   that treats `UNDECIDABLE` as still-in-population, R2's own numbers become far weaker than the
   headline branch.
2. **No verbatim prompt log is committed.** `RULE.md` §3's phrase "quoted to the readers as context
   and to nothing else" is grammatically attached to the claim sentence, and it is genuinely
   ambiguous whether the *worked example* quoted just before it — "A paper about self-verification
   in code generation is evidence about code generation. Counting it... inflates the denominator" —
   was also shown to the readers, or only to a human auditing `RULE.md`. If it was shown, the
   readers were not given a bare criterion; they were given the criterion *plus a worked example of
   the exact failure the disagreement later clusters on* (subject-matter vs. system). That would be
   a real thumb on the scale, in the direction the result went. Nothing in the repository resolves
   this either way.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY SUCCEEDS. NOT BLOCKING** for the core reproducibility claim (the criterion and
source material are fairly matched). **BLOCKING** for the "gets stronger" claim specifically (see
S4, where the `UNDECIDABLE` asymmetry turns out to matter a lot) and worth fixing going forward:
commit the actual prompt text, not just RULE.md's description of it.

## S3 (attack C) — is the one-way direction actually informative?

**What I did.** Modelled the "zero flips OUT→IN" fact under a null of symmetric noise: if the true
per-case probability that an *excluded* case gets flipped IN by an independent reader is `p`, what
is `P(0 flips in 21 trials)`, for both readers independently?

**What I found:**

| assumed symmetric flip rate p | P(0/21, one reader) | P(0/21, **both** readers) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.02 | 0.654 | 0.428 |
| 0.05 | 0.341 | 0.116 |
| 0.10 | 0.109 | 0.012 |
| 0.15 | 0.033 | 0.001 |
| 0.20 | 0.009 | 0.0001 |

Even at a symmetric flip rate of **3.6 %** — one-tenth of R2's own observed IN→OUT rate (35.9 %) —
zero flips across both readers on 21 cases is still the *modal, more-likely-than-not* outcome
(46 %). Only once the assumed symmetric rate approaches the observed one-way rate itself does "zero"
become improbable. Separately, a qualitative read of the 21 excluded reasons (`build_data.py`
`EXCLUDED`) finds no case that reads as a plausible IN-candidate to begin with — robotic planning,
Diplomacy negotiation, Chinese legalese style, grade-school word problems, theorem proving. There
was structurally little room for a symmetric flip to occur even if the readers' true error rate
were symmetric.

**This does not touch the core finding** (39 does not reproduce as 23; that's a fact about the
IN-population, not about the OUT-population's stability). It does directly undercut the specific,
separately-asserted claim in `FINDINGS.md`: *"It is not that the original reader was noisy — noise
is symmetric... The original is strictly more inclusive, in one direction."* That sentence treats
zero observed flips as proof against symmetric noise; the arithmetic above shows zero flips is
uninformative at any but a fairly high symmetric rate, and the pool gives noise little material to
act on regardless.

**Verdict: SUCCEEDS against the "not noise — direction" argument specifically. BLOCKING** for that
sentence as stated (it should read as "consistent with a real asymmetry" rather than proof against
symmetric noise). **NOT BLOCKING** for the core n-shift finding, which does not depend on this
argument.

## S4 (attack D) — is "gets stronger" a selection from small numbers?

**What I did.** Took the four re-split branches `results.json` actually computes (R1/R2 ×
undecidable-in/out) and (a) computed how much the reported ratio moves for a ±1 swing in the
blind-reader `contextualizes` count (the denominator, which is 3, 4, 5, or 10 across the four
branches), and (b) tested, branch by branch, whether the blind-reader `contextualizes` rate under
each new split is statistically distinguishable from the published rate (14/39 = 35.9 %), via
Fisher's exact test (a conservative, approximate bound — the samples are not fully independent
since they are overlapping subsets of the same 60 cases, but the comparison is directionally
informative and no more approximate than the ratio claim it is checking).

**What I found:**

| branch | ratio (machine ÷ blind) | ratio at denom −1 | ratio at denom +1 | Fisher p vs. published rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| published (n=39) | 2.29 | — | — | — |
| R1, undecidable outside (n=23) | **6.33** | 4.75 | 9.50 | 0.077 |
| R2, undecidable outside (n=23) | **5.00** | 4.00 | 6.67 | 0.154 |
| R1, undecidable inside (n=26) | 4.20 | 3.50 | 5.25 | 0.174 |
| R2, undecidable inside (n=31) | **2.60** | 2.36 | 2.89 | **0.804** |

Two things follow. First, the ratio is genuinely fragile: with a denominator of 3 or 4, one case
either way swings the headline number by 25–50 %. Second — and this is the sharper point — **none**
of the four branches shows the blind-reader `contextualizes` rate moving from the published rate at
conventional significance, and the weakest case is not close: R2's own undecidable-inside branch
(2.60 vs. 2.29, p = 0.804) is statistically indistinguishable from what was already published. That
branch is not a hypothetical the correction glosses over lightly — it is one of the two branches
`DEVIATIONS.md` D1 pre-registers as equally valid, produced by the same reader (R2), under the same
rule. `FINDINGS.md` and `CORRECTIONS.md` both lead with the outside-branch numbers ("roughly
doubled," "4.2 or 6.3×"...  displayed prominently) and relegate the inside branch to "the other
branch is in results.json and DEVIATIONS.md D1; the finding does not turn on it" — which is true for
the Band C verdict (n still moves > 5 either way) but is not true for the "gets stronger" claim,
which turns on it substantially: under R2's most original-favourable, equally pre-registered
reading, the strengthening all but disappears.

**Verdict: SUCCEEDS. BLOCKING** for "the finding... is roughly doubled" / "gets stronger" as a
general statement — it should be scoped to "in the least favourable branch to the original, the gap
widens by 2–3×; in the most favourable, equally pre-registered branch, it does not move outside
noise." **NOT BLOCKING** for the core reproducibility claim, which does not rest on the ratio.

## S5 (attack E) — does Band C actually bind?

**What I did.** Verified, from git history rather than from the documents' own narration, that
`RULE.md` (with its Band A/B/C thresholds), `blind-input.json`, `score.py`, and `selftest.py` were
all committed before either reader file existed:

```
9417b3e 15:36:06  RULE.md + blind-input.json
cae69e2 15:40:25  score.py
9c6d3d4 15:42:09  selftest.py (21 assertions)
a724046 15:43:55  reader-R1.json
d6d52d6 15:45:49  reader-R2.json
```

That timing claim is accurate — the bands were genuinely fixed before the numbers existed. Then I
asked the substantive version of the attack: given base rates, was Band C ever *not* going to fire?
Band C fires on `n moves by more than 5`. To stay under that, an independent reader would need to
reproduce at least 34 of the original's 39 IN calls (≥ 87 %) net. The original work's own
Interlocutor (I1) already cites this practice's own prior literature review putting LLM-judge
agreement on comparable subjective classification at κ ≈ 0.19–0.51 — a regime that essentially never
produces 87 % net reproduction on a 39-item subjective population judgement, independent of whether
the specific 39 was good or bad.

**What I found.** The threshold is honestly pre-registered but is not a demanding one relative to
known base rates for this kind of task: it was highly likely to fire on *any* real second reading,
which means "Band C fired" is weak evidence, on its own, that this *particular* split was
defective — it is closer to evidence that "no LLM-judged 60-item subjective split reproduces above
87 %," a much less interesting claim already conceded elsewhere in this practice's record. The
diagnostic content is in the magnitude and direction of the actual numbers (independently checked in
S1–S4), not in the fact that a threshold built to catch ordinary disagreement caught ordinary
disagreement.

**Verdict: PARTIALLY SUCCEEDS. NOT BLOCKING** — the pre-registration is genuine and not gamed; what
it does not support is treating "Band C fired" as itself a finding rather than a trigger for the
disclosure the rule was designed to force.

## S6 (attack F, and my own additions) — reuser reach, sibling key, the Fisher test

**What I checked, each independently:**

1. **The `jq` sibling-key claim.** `CORRECTIONS.md` states `jq '.cases[] | select(.in_population)'`
   still returns 39 with no visible sign of the correction. Ran it: **confirmed, still 39.** The
   limitation is disclosed accurately, not minimized.
2. **Data integrity of the correction.** `CORRECTIONS.md` claims 0 pre-existing leaves changed, only
   new keys added. Diffed `data.json` at the pre-correction commit (`1949ea6`) against HEAD
   programmatically, per-case, per-key: **0 changed leaves among the original fields, confirmed
   independently** — not just by re-reading the practice's own count.
3. **`score.py` / `selftest.py` reproducibility.** Re-ran both from scratch: 21/21 selftest
   assertions pass; `score.py` regenerates `results.json` byte-for-byte matching what's committed,
   including κ = 0.9602, both Band C verdicts, and all four re-split tables. **The mechanical core
   of this study is sound; no arithmetic or logic error was found anywhere in it.**
4. **The Fisher exact "p = 0.039" post-hoc characterization.** This is the one number in the whole
   study with no committed script. `FINDINGS.md` states: 13 of the 39 included titles carry a marker
   word (bench/benchmark/evaluation/survey/toolkit/audit/identifiers/arena/suite), both readers kept
   only 4 of those 13 against 18 of the 26 unmarked, Fisher p = 0.039. I tried three independent,
   equally reasonable reconstructions of "marking... for the words [list]" against the title field:
   a stemmed/broad match (14 marked, 5 kept, p = 0.091), a strict whole-word match (10 marked, 2
   kept, p = 0.011), and a substring match (12 marked, 3 kept, p = 0.014). **None reproduces 13
   marked / 4 kept / p = 0.039**, and the three reasonable methods span p = 0.011 to p = 0.091 —
   crossing the conventional 0.05 line in both directions depending on matching method. The
   direction of the effect (marked titles kept less often) replicates under every method I tried;
   the specific number and its significance do not. This is exactly the researcher-degrees-of-
   freedom problem this session's own pre-registration exists to guard against, applied to the one
   analysis in the study that was explicitly exempted from pre-registration ("post-hoc, labelled as
   such"). The label is honest about *when* the analysis was chosen; it does not make the number
   independently checkable, and in a directory otherwise built entirely from committed, self-tested,
   timestamped scripts, the absence of one here for the one number doing rhetorical heavy lifting
   ("if that reading is right, this work made... the error its own docstring warns against") stands
   out.

**Verdict:** S6.1–S6.3 **FAIL as attacks** (the claims they test are accurate and independently
verified). S6.4 **SUCCEEDS. BLOCKING** for the Fisher-test sentence specifically — it should either
carry a committed script and a stated matching method, or be cut back to "marked titles were kept
less often by both readers" without the unreproducible p-value. **NOT BLOCKING** for anything else,
since both documents already call it "a characterisation, not a test" that "does not carry the
finding."

## What the claim is reduced to

After all six attacks, the part that survives intact and independently re-executed:

> Two readers, convened blind to the published split and to each other, under a rule and thresholds
> committed to git before either saw a case, each independently returned 23 of 60 — not 39 — and
> agreed with each other far more (κ = 0.96, 82 % raw agreement even restricted to the 39 contested
> cases) than either agreed with the published split (κ = 0.54 / 0.70). Every one of the original's
> 21 exclusions was confirmed by both readers; all disagreement ran the other way. Under every one
> of the four pre-registered re-split branches, n moves by more than 5, which is what the
> pre-registered rule (honestly timestamped before the readers ran) defines as requiring a dated
> correction — and that correction was made, reaches the served page and the data (with the
> pre-correction split's own limits disclosed, not just the new one's), and changes no previously
> published value. **"32 of 39 (82 %)" does not survive as computed; the population it was computed
> over was not independently reproduced.**

What should be pulled back, stated with the qualifications this review adds:

- "The population judgement... reproduces" holds only in the sense the work's own §10 already
  states: two roles from the same practice and technology family agree with each other, not that
  the judgement has been checked against an outside reader. No metadata even records whether R1 and
  R2 are draws from the same or different models — a one-line gap worth closing before this design
  is reused.
- "Noise is symmetric... the original is strictly more inclusive, in one direction" overstates what
  zero flips on 21 largely-unambiguous exclusions can prove; a modest symmetric error rate would
  produce exactly this pattern by chance more often than not.
- "The finding gets stronger" / "roughly doubles" is true of the two more dramatic branches (R1 and
  R2 with undecidables held outside the population) and not meaningfully true of R2's own
  undecidable-inside branch (2.60 vs. published 2.29, statistically indistinguishable) — a branch
  the study's own deviation log treats as equally valid. State the range, not the top of it.
- The Fisher p = 0.039 should not be quoted without a script behind it; independent reconstruction
  puts it anywhere from 0.011 to 0.091 depending on defensible matching choices.

None of these four qualifications reopen the headline number. They narrow the story around it from
"the correction costs a number and hands back a strengthened, well-characterized finding" to "the
correction costs a number and hands back the same underlying finding, whose size under
re-measurement ranges from unchanged to roughly doubled depending on a branch this study itself
left open, and whose one piece of supporting characterization is not independently checkable."
