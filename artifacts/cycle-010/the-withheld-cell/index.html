The Withheld Cell

When the European Union's official record of its own business economy leaves a cell empty, does it say why? And when it says confidential, whose economy goes missing?

What this first version says

Method

One table: Eurostat's Enterprises by detailed NACE Rev. 2 activity and special aggregates (sbs_ovw_act), reference year 2021, read from the dissemination API as JSON-stat (fetch.sh; the digest of the copy analysed is in data/results.json). The universe is the 27 member states × NACE codes at division, group and class level × 19 level indicators (counts and money amounts; ratios are left out because they inherit their numerators' fate). A cell is published if it carries a value, withheld if Eurostat flags it C (confidential), and silent if it carries neither. No withheld cell carries a value. The code is analyse.py; it reproduces every number on this page from the fetched file.

Limits

One year, one table. A withheld cell may be withheld to protect a firm (primary confidentiality) or to stop a reader subtracting it out of a total (secondary). The flag does not say which, so this page cannot tell the two apart. Country rates count cells, not euros: a withheld cell in a tiny class weighs the same as one in a large class. The size measure is a count of enterprises, not of output. Eurostat revises the table, so a later fetch may differ.

Literature

The rule. European law defines confidential data as "data which allow statistical units to be identified, either directly or indirectly, thereby disclosing individual information" (Regulation (EC) No 223/2009, Art. 3(7)). Eurostat's metadata for this table gives two reasons to withhold a cell. The first is too few contributors: a cell is withheld when "the extrapolated number of contributors is positive but less than a specified number that is referred to as the 'minimum threshold'". The second is one firm too large to hide: "the second largest contributor, can estimate the value of the largest contributor within a chosen percentage, p%". A cell is "secondary confidential if it can be used with marginal totals to disclose primary confidential cells", and "the thresholds are defined for each country" (Eurostat SBS metadata, §7). Figure 1 shows the first reason at work. Ireland may be showing the second, but this page does not yet test that.

Has it been measured before? Partly, by the producers themselves. Eurostat's national metadata pages report a rate of confidential cells by dataset and activity level. Estonia's page writes that "as Estonia is a small country the rate of confidential cells is considerable", and for 2020 it lists 0.0 % at section level against 11.6 to 16.4 % at division, group and class in its first dataset (Estonia, §7). So the per-country rate is not new. We have not yet found the comparison across all 27 on one disseminated table, or withholding set against the count of firms. The official-statistics journals have not yet been searched directly. Passages: data/sources.json. Nothing here is cited from memory.
